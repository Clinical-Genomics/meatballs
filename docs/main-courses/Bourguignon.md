# Boeuf Bourguignon 

## Ingridient 
-  1.5 kg beef chuck or braising steak, cut into large chunks (about 4–5 cm)
- 200 g smoked bacon lardons or thick-cut bacon, diced
- 1 large brown onion, finely diced
- 2 garlic cloves, crushed or minced
- 3 tbsp plain flour (all-purpose flour)
- 1 bottle (750 ml) dry red wine (preferably a Burgundy Pinot Noir)
- 500 ml rich beef stock or broth
- 1 tbsp tomato purée (tomato paste)
- 2 bay leaves
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 tbsp olive oil or butter for searing

## Instructions

1. Brown the bacon and beef:
	• Heat a large, heavy-bottomed Dutch oven or cast-iron casserole over medium heat. Add the bacon lardons and cook until crisp and golden. Remove the bacon with a slotted spoon and set aside, leaving the fat in the pot.
	• Pat the beef chunks dry with paper towels (this is essential for a good sear) and season with salt and pepper. Working in batches, brown the beef on all sides in the hot bacon fat and oil. Remove the beef and set aside with the bacon.
1. Aromatics and flour:
	• In the same pot, add the diced onion and cook for 3–5 minutes until softened. Add the garlic and cook for 1 more minute.
	• Sprinkle the flour over the onions and stir well, cooking for 1–2 minutes to get rid of the raw flour taste.
3. Combine and simmer:
	• Gradually pour in the red wine and the beef stock, scraping up any delicious browned bits from the bottom of the pot with a wooden spoon.
	• Stir in the tomato purée, bay leaves, and thyme sprigs. Return the beef and bacon (along with any resting juices) back into the pot. Bring to a gentle simmer.
	• Cover with a tight-fitting lid and transfer to the oven preheated to 160°C (150°C fan) / Gas 3. Let it braise slowly for about 1.5 to 2 hours.
4. Prepare the garnish:
	• While the stew is in the oven, heat the butter and oil in a separate frying pan. Sauté the pearl onions and mushrooms until they are golden brown and tender (about 10 minutes).
5. Finish the stew:
	• Remove the Dutch oven from the oven. Stir in the sautéed pearl onions and mushrooms.
	• Cover and return to the oven for another 30 to 45 minutes, or until the beef is fork-tender and the sauce has thickened nicely. If the sauce is too thin, you can simmer it uncovered on the stovetop for a few minutes.
	• Discard the bay leaves and thyme stems.
6. Serve:
	• Scatter with fresh chopped parsley and serve hot over creamy mashed potatoes, buttered noodles, or crusty French bread.
